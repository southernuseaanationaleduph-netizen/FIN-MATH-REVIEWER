II. Independent and dependent events

Ask one question: does knowing that one event happened change the probability of the other?

Independent

P(B | A) = P(B)   or   P(A | B) = P(A)

  • Tossing a coin twice
  • Rolling a die and tossing a coin
  • Drawing two cards with replacement
  • Two machines that work separately

Dependent

P(B | A) ≠ P(B)   or   P(A | B) ≠ P(A)

  • Drawing two cards without replacement
  • Choosing a President, then a Vice-President from the same class
  • Raffle tickets that are not returned to the drum
  • Being a senior and being a Robotics Club member (when the data say the probabilities change)
Trap to remember. Mutually exclusive is not the same as independent. If two events with non-zero probabilities cannot occur together, then P(A ∩ B) = 0 ≠ P(A)·P(B), so they are dependent.

IV. Solving for missing values

Write the formula, substitute what you know, then rearrange for the unknown.

UnknownRearranged formula
P(A ∩ B) from a unionP(A ∩ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∪ B)
P(B) from a conditionalP(B) = {P(A ∩ B)//P(A | B)}
P(B) when independentP(B) = {P(A ∩ B)//P(A)}
P(A) in the Bayes denominatorP(A) = P(B)·P(A | B) + P(B′)·P(A | B′)
P(A | B′) from total probabilityP(A | B′) = {P(A) − P(B)·P(A | B)//P(B′)}

Algebraic variables

  • Complements and sums: write x + (3x − 0.20) = 1, collect like terms, solve for x.
  • Mutually exclusive events: P(A) + P(B) = P(A ∪ B).
  • Independent events: P(A)·P(B) = P(A ∩ B). This may give x² = a number, so take the positive root.
  • After solving, substitute x back and check that every probability is between 0 and 1.

V. Which rule should I use?

Read the key words in the problem.

Key wordsRule
“and”, “both”, “first … then …”, “one after the other”Multiplication rule P(A ∩ B) = P(A)·P(B | A). Without replacement: use the reduced counts. With replacement or “independent”: P(A)·P(B).
“or”, “either”, “at least one of the two”Addition rule P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B)
“given that”, “if it is known that”, “among those who”Conditional probability P(A | B) = {P(A ∩ B)//P(B)}. In a table, only the given row or column is the sample space.
The result is known, you want the source or cause (defective item, positive test)Bayes' rule. Compute the total probability in the denominator first.
“not”, “neither”, “none”Complement P(A′) = 1 − P(A)
Two or three overlapping groups, “only”, “exactly one”, “neither”Venn diagram. Fill the overlap first, then the “only” regions, then the outside.
